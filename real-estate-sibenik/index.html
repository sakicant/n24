    Living in Šibenik

    Šibenik is the centre of the county and the largest town between Split and Zadar. Its old town of stone streets climbs from the waterfront to the fortresses, and the Cathedral of St James and St Nicholas Fortress are on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

    As a real city, Šibenik has everything open all year: a hospital, schools, shops and services. Krka National Park is a short drive inland.

    Houses, villas and apartments in Šibenik

    Šibenik offers the widest choice of property on this coast: apartments in the old town and in newer neighbourhoods, family houses, villas on the edge of town and new builds near the sea.

    Building land in Šibenik

    Building land in and around Šibenik ranges from small plots in residential areas to larger plots for development. Ownership records here are often complex, so cadastre and land registry checks are especially important.

    Buying property in Šibenik: questions

    Is Šibenik a good place to live all year?

    Yes. Šibenik is a working city with a hospital, schools, shops and services open all year, which makes it the most practical choice on this coast for people who want to live here permanently.

    Can foreigners buy property in Šibenik?

    Citizens of EU countries can buy property in Croatia under the same conditions as Croatian citizens. For citizens of other countries it depends on an agreement between Croatia and their country and on approval by the Ministry of Justice. Tomislav will check your case before you make an offer.

    What should I check before buying land in Šibenik?

    Check that the plot lies inside the building zone of the spatial plan, that the land registry and the cadastre match, who all the owners are, and that the plot has road access and utilities. N24 checks all of this for the land it sells.
